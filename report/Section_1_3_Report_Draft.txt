1.3 Assessment of Data Quality
------------------------------
Prior to statistical imputation and feature transformation, we conducted a
comprehensive syntactic and semantic audit across all 13,843 seller profiles
and 33 attributes. No duplicate records, duplicate seller identifiers (sp_id),
or negative numerical values were found. All proportion attributes (pct_isCab,
pct_is_new, pct_salvage, pct_theft_title) strictly lie within the closed
interval [0, 1], advertised discounts never exceed vehcle prices
(savings_amount_median < price_median), and temporal consistency holds across
all records (n_listing_days <= n_listings and n_listing_days <= listing_span_days + 1).
Furthermore, fractional ".5" values observed in count and calendar-year medians
(year_median, owner_count_median, daysonmarket_median) were verified to be
mathematically valid medians resulting from sellers with an even number of
vehicle listings. Finally, the unique seller identifier sp_id (originally
stored as float64, e.g., 404771.0) was stripped of its decimal suffix via
integer casting and stored as a nominal string (object) identifier
(.astype(int).astype(str)) so it is automatically excluded from numerical
summary statistics before being dropped in Section 1.3.4.


1.3.1 Missing Value Handling
----------------------------
Out of the 33 initial attributes, 20 attributes contain missing information—17
with explicit NaN values and 3 categorical attributes containing the string
literal "unknown"—while the remaining 13 attributes are 100% complete (see
Table 1.3 and Figure 1.3: Missing Value Heatmap).

Visual inspection of the missingness matrix and row-level indices reveals that
missing values are not purely random, but exhibit structured, domain-specific
block co-missingness:
  * Title-History Block (27 sellers, 0.20%): pct_isCab, pct_salvage, and
    pct_theft_title are missing on the exact same 27 records, corresponding to
    dealers whose listings lacked integrated vehicle-history reports (e.g.,
    Carfax/AutoCheck).
  * EPA Fuel-Economy Block (16 sellers, 0.12%): city_fuel_economy_median and
    highway_fuel_economy_median are missing on the exact same 16 records,
    reflecting dealers specializing in heavy-duty commercial trucks or
    specialty vehicles exempt from standard EPA MPG ratings.
  * Factory Specification-Sheet Block (6–7 sellers, 0.04–0.05%): Physical
    chassis dimensions (height_median, length_median, width_median) and
    horsepower_median are missing on the exact same 6 records (which also
    overlap with the 7 records missing front_legroom_median and
    fuel_tank_volume_median), indicating unrecorded specification sheets.

Because complete-case (row-wise) deletion would discard over 76% of all
sellers, we applied tailored column-level handling strategies:

  * dominant_wheel_system (75.98% missing, 10,518 rows) -> Feature Elimination:
    Since more than three-quarters of the dataset lacks drivetrain records, any
    imputation method would fabricate the majority of the column and severely
    bias downstream clustering and classification. We therefore dropped this
    attribute.
  * vehicle_age_median (1 missing row, 0.01%) -> Mathematical Imputation:
    For seller sp_id = 293033 (where year_median = 2020.0), the missing value
    was deterministically recovered using the exact domain relationship
    vehicle_age_median = 2020.0 - year_median = 0.0 years.
  * Remaining 14 Minor Missing Numerical Attributes (<0.30% missing, 1–39 rows)
    -> 1D Group-Median Imputation by dominant_body_type:
    Minor missing values in owner_count_median (39), title-history ratios (27),
    back_legroom_median (22, including the 3 erroneous zeros recoded to NaN),
    fuel economy (16), front legroom and fuel tank volume (7), dimensions and
    horsepower_median (6), and mileage_median (1) were imputed using the median
    within each dominant_body_type segment, ensuring robustness against
    right-skewed distributions while retaining all 13,843 sellers.
  * engine_displacement_median (24.51% missing, 3,393 rows) -> 2D Stratified
    Group-Median Imputation by (dominant_body_type, hp_bin):
    At 24.51% missingness, dropping the column would forfeit key powertrain
    information. However, 1D group-median imputation by dominant_body_type
    alone is insufficient because suv_crossover (65.2%) and sedan (20.8%)
    comprise 86.0% of all sellers and share the exact same median (2,500.0 cc),
    which would lump 2,936 out of 3,393 missing records (86.5%) into a single
    artificial spike at 2,500.0 cc and shrink the IQR from 1,100 cc to 800 cc.
    Because engine displacement is strongly coupled with horsepower
    (Spearman r_s = +0.77) and horsepower_median is 99.96% complete, we
    partitioned sellers into 5 horsepower quintiles (hp_bin: [78, 185],
    (185, 216.5], (216.5, 255], (255, 287], and (287, 630] HP) and imputed
    missing values using the median of each (dominant_body_type, hp_bin) cell
    (with fallback to dominant_body_type median). On an 80/20 holdout
    validation split of the 10,450 observed records, this 2D imputation reduces
    Mean Absolute Error (MAE) from 622.6 cc (global median) and 521.3 cc (1D
    body-type median) down to 349.3 cc (a 44.9% error reduction), distributing
    imputed values across 22 realistic engine sizes (2,000–5,650 cc) and
    preserving the original IQR of 1,100 cc (Q1 = 2,400 cc, Q3 = 3,500 cc).
  * Implicit "unknown" in Categorical Attributes:
    For dominant_fuel_type (1 row, 0.01%) and dominant_transmission (6 rows,
    0.04%), "unknown" strings were recoded as NaN and imputed with the mode
    grouped by dominant_body_type ("gasoline" and "a", respectively). Conversely,
    for dominant_listing_color (1,510 rows, 10.91%), because the top two colors
    ("white" at 37.3% and "black" at 32.3%) have comparable shares, mode
    imputation would artificially inflate "white" by nearly 11%. Thus,
    "unknown" is retained as a legitimate behavioral category representing
    sellers that do not report exterior color.

As demonstrated in Table 1.4, indiscriminately removing all records flagged by
the univariate 1.5*IQR rule across numerical attributes would eliminate 6,158
out of 13,843 sellers (44.5% of the dataset). Closer inspection reveals two
structural reasons why the naive IQR rule produces false-positive outlier flags:
  1. Zero-Inflated Risk Proportions: In pct_theft_title (15.24% flagged) and
     pct_salvage (8.97% flagged), over 84% of sellers have a value of 0.0,
     resulting in Q1 = Q3 = 0 and IQR = 0. Consequently, any seller with a
     non-zero share of salvage or theft-title vehicles (>0) falls above the
     upper fence, even though these represent a genuine segment of secondary
     and damaged-vehicle dealers.
  2. Tightly Standardized Ergonomic & Dimension Specs: Mainstream passenger
     sedans and SUVs cluster tightly around industry norms (e.g., 98% of
     sellers have front_legroom_median within 40.8–43.9 in, IQR = 0.90 in).
     Sellers specializing in heavy-duty commercial pickup trucks (fuel tank up
     to 52.0 gal, length up to 282.3 in) or high-roof commercial cargo vans
     (height_median up to 107.5 in) naturally fall outside the narrow fences.

We therefore distinguished true measurement noise from valid domain outliers:
  * True Semantic Noise Elimination (back_legroom_median == 0.0):
    In Section 1.1.3, we identified 3 records (sp_id = 350763, 353464, 309061)
    with back_legroom_median == 0.0 in despite having dominant_body_type
    recorded as multi-seat suv_crossover or sedan. Because 0.0 in rear legroom
    is physically impossible for multi-row passenger vehicles, these 3 values
    were recoded as NaN and imputed with the body-type median in Section 1.3.1,
    raising the minimum to a realistic 18.80 in.
  * Retention of Valid Domain Outliers & Tail-Compression Strategy:
    Extreme right-tail values in business volume (n_listings up to 4,959 for
    mega-dealership groups), pricing (price_median up to $236,438 and
    horsepower_median up to 630 HP for luxury/exotic dealers), vehicle age
    (up to 60 years for classic car specialists), fuel economy (up to 126 MPG
    representing EPA MPGe for electric/hybrid dealers), and turnover
    (daysonmarket_median up to 1,987 days for slow-moving collector inventory)
    reflect real market heterogeneity. Rather than deleting or arbitrarily
    clipping these sellers—which would erase niche market segments—we retain
    all 13,843 records in the cleaned dataset and apply logarithmic tail
    compression (np.log1p) in Section 1.3.3. For algorithms highly sensitive to
    extreme Euclidean distances (such as K-Means in Module 2), localized
    percentile filtering or robust scaling can be applied in task-specific
    pipelines without compromising the integrity of the master dataset.


1.3.3 Variables Transformation
------------------------------
To address severe right-skewness and resolve magnitude disparities across the
numerical attributes, we applied two sequential transformations: logarithmic
tail compression (np.log1p) and feature scaling (Z-Score Standardization and
Min-Max Normalization).

1. Logarithmic Transformation (np.log1p) for Right-Skewed Features:
   Fifteen non-negative numerical attributes exhibit pronounced positive
   skewness (Skewness > 1.2) in the cleaned dataset. Because several attributes
   (savings_amount_median, daysonmarket_median, mileage_median,
   vehicle_age_median) contain valid zero observations where log(0) is
   undefined, we applied log(1 + x) via np.log1p.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

   As shown in Table 1.5, np.log1p dramatically compresses extreme right tails
   and restores near-symmetry (|Skewness| < 1.0) across all 9 unbounded
   business-scale, pricing, turnover, age, and powertrain variables—for
   example, reducing skewness in n_listings from +5.25 to -0.13,
   daysonmarket_median from +8.75 to +0.47, price_median from +3.45 to -0.45,
   engine_displacement_median from +1.26 to +0.39, and vehicle_age_median from
   +2.32 to 0.00. Conversely, for bounded proportions in [0, 1] with heavy
   point masses at zero (pct_theft_title, pct_salvage, pct_isCab), log(1 + x)
   behaves nearly linearly (ln(1+x) ~ x for small x) and cannot remove
   structural zero-inflation; these proportions are best kept on their native
   [0, 1] scale or discretized into categorical bins for Pattern Mining.

2. Feature Scaling (StandardScaler vs. MinMaxScaler):
   In the raw feature space, attributes span vastly different scales:
   mileage_median ([0, 238,541]) and price_median ([1,699, 236,438]) are five
   orders of magnitude larger than proportion variables ([0, 1]). Without
   scaling, distance-based algorithms (K-Means, DBSCAN, Hierarchical
   Clustering, and KNN) would be entirely dominated by mileage and price. We
   therefore generated both Z-Score Standardized (StandardScaler, mean = 0,
   std = 1) and Min-Max Normalized (MinMaxScaler, [0, 1]) representations.
   Notably, standardizing untransformed skewed features still leaves extreme
   maxima (e.g., +35.32 std for n_listings and +34.57 std for
   daysonmarket_median), confirming that np.log1p compression must precede
   Z-score standardization when preparing inputs for distance-based models.


1.3.4 Pairwise Correlations and Variable Elimination
----------------------------------------------------
To investigate inter-feature dependencies, we first visualized a Pairwise
Scatter Plot Matrix across key numerical attributes colored by
seller_rating_class (Figure 1.5), and subsequently computed the full Spearman
rank correlation matrix (r_s) across all 24 numerical variables (Figure 1.6 and
Table 1.6).

We selected Spearman's rank correlation (r_s) over Pearson's linear correlation
(r_p) for two reasons verified by Figure 1.5:
  1. Non-Linear Monotonic Relationships: Scatter plots of price_median against
     mileage_median and vehicle_age_median follow an exponential L-shaped
     depreciation curve—prices drop steeply over the first 3–5 years (or first
     50,000 miles) and flatten asymptotically for older vehicles. Spearman
     accurately captures this monotonic decay without assuming linearity.
  2. Robustness to Extreme Tails: Spearman operates on ranks and is invariant
     to extreme right-tail outliers and monotonic log transformations.

Key Insights from the Correlation Structure & Variable Elimination Decisions:

  1. Elimination of Near-Duplicate Collinear Features (|r_s| > 0.93):
     * year_median <-> vehicle_age_median (r_s = -0.9837): Because
       vehicle_age_median is a direct linear shift of year_median
       (2020.0 - year_median), keeping both introduces near-singular
       multicollinearity in regression and double-weights vehicle age in
       clustering. We dropped year_median and retained vehicle_age_median
       (which is a true ratio-scale variable starting at 0.0 with 0.00 log1p
       skewness).
     * city_fuel_economy_median <-> highway_fuel_economy_median (r_s = +0.9356):
       Both variables form a tight linear diagonal across conventional ICE and
       EV/hybrid inventories, capturing the same powertrain efficiency
       dimension. We dropped highway_fuel_economy_median and retained
       city_fuel_economy_median.
     * n_listings <-> n_listing_days (r_s = +0.9559): Total inventory size and
       active posting days are strongly coupled. While n_listing_days is kept
       in the general cleaned table for exploratory flexibility (e.g., deriving
       posting intensity), it is excluded alongside redundant variables when
       constructing non-collinear feature sets for clustering and regression.

  2. Distinct Domain Correlation Blocks (0.71 <= |r_s| <= 0.93):
     * Vehicle Condition & Lifecycle Block: vehicle_age_median, mileage_median
       (r_s = +0.9268), pct_is_new (r_s = -0.8671), price_median
       (r_s = -0.8154), and owner_count_median (r_s = +0.7926) form a cohesive
       lifecycle cluster. Although mileage_median and vehicle_age_median are
       strongly correlated, both are retained because their divergence captures
       annual usage intensity (miles driven per year), which is highly
       informative for price regression and rating classification.
     * Physical Size & Powertrain Block: fuel_tank_volume_median correlates
       positively with horsepower_median (+0.8049), engine_displacement_median
       (+0.7949), and length_median (+0.7596), and negatively with
       city_fuel_economy_median (-0.7977), while engine_displacement_median
       also correlates strongly with horsepower_median (+0.7653) and
       city_fuel_economy_median (-0.7626), cleanly separating heavy truck/SUV
       dealers from compact sedan/hatchback specialists.

  3. Final Prepared Dataset Summary (13,843 rows x 29 columns):
     Removing dominant_wheel_system (75.98% missing in Section 1.3.1) together
     with sp_id (non-predictive identifier), year_median, and
     highway_fuel_economy_median yields a clean, zero-NaN master dataset of
     13,843 sellers and 29 attributes. Furthermore, because the categorical
     attribute new_used_profile is deterministically derived from pct_is_new
     (verified with 100% agreement in Section 1.1.3), downstream models use
     pct_is_new for numerical modeling and new_used_profile for categorical
     Pattern Mining without including both simultaneously.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{\texttt{dominant\_wheel\_system} ($75.98\%$ missing):} Dropped from the dataset, as imputing three-quarters of the column would synthesize artificial drivetrain distributions.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{vehicle\_age\_median} ($1$ missing):} For seller \texttt{sp\_id = 293033} ($\texttt{year\_median} = 2020.0$), the missing value was deterministically recovered as $\texttt{vehicle\_age\_median} = 2020.0 - \texttt{year\_median} = 0.0\text{ years}$.
  \item \textbf{Minor missing numerical attributes ($14$ columns, $<0.30\%$ missing):} Imputed via the median segmented by \texttt{dominant\_body\_type}, ensuring robustness to right-skewness while preserving all $13,843$ records.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{engine\_displacement\_median} ($24.51\%$ missing, $3,393$ rows):} Imputed using a \textbf{2D stratified group median by \texttt{(dominant\_body\_type, hp\_bin)}}, where \texttt{hp\_bin} partitions \texttt{horsepower\_median} into $5$ quintiles. Because \texttt{suv\_crossover} ($65.2\%$) and \texttt{sedan} ($20.8\%$) share the same overall median ($2,500\text{ cc}$), 1D imputation by \texttt{dominant\_body\_type} alone would lump $86.5\%$ of missing rows ($2,936/3,393$) into a single spike at $2,500\text{ cc}$ and shrink the IQR from $1,100\text{ cc}$ to $800\text{ cc}$. Conditioning on both body type and horsepower quintiles distributes the imputed values across $22$ realistic engine sizes ($2,000\text{--}5,650\text{ cc}$), preserves the original IQR of $1,100\text{ cc}$, and reduces $80/20$ holdout validation MAE from $622.6\text{ cc}$ (global median) and $521.3\text{ cc}$ (1D body-type median) down to $349.3\text{ cc}$ ($-44.9\%$ error).
  \item \textbf{Implicit \texttt{"unknown"} values:} Negligible \texttt{"unknown"} counts in \texttt{dominant\_fuel\_type} ($1$ row) and \texttt{dominant\_transmission} ($6$ rows) were replaced with the segment mode (\texttt{"gasoline"} and \texttt{"a"}). In \texttt{dominant\_listing\_color} ($1,510$ rows, $10.91\%$), \texttt{"unknown"} is retained as a valid category because mode imputation would artificially inflate the top color (\texttt{"white"}, $37.3\%$) by nearly $11\%$.
\end{itemize}

Removing all records flagged by the univariate $1.5 \times \text{IQR}$ rule across numerical features would discard $6,158$ out of $13,843$ sellers ($44.5\%$). This high flag rate stems from two structural properties: (1) \textbf{zero-inflated risk proportions} (\texttt{pct\_theft\_title} and \texttt{pct\_salvage}), where over $84\%$ of sellers equal $0.0$ ($Q_1 = Q_3 = 0, \text{IQR} = 0$), causing every non-zero value to be flagged; and (2) \textbf{tightly standardized passenger-car dimensions} (e.g., $\text{IQR} = 0.90\text{ in}$ for \texttt{front\_legroom\_median}), which flag legitimate commercial truck and cargo-van dealers.

Accordingly, we separated \textbf{true semantic noise} from \textbf{valid domain outliers}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{True semantic noise (\texttt{back\_legroom\_median == 0.0}):} Three records exhibited $0.0\text{ in}$ rear legroom despite being multi-seat \texttt{suv\_crossover} or \texttt{sedan} profiles. Because $0.0\text{ in}$ rear legroom is physically impossible for multi-row passenger vehicles, these $3$ values were converted to \texttt{NaN} and imputed via group medians in Section~\ref{sec:missing} (raising \texttt{Min} to $18.80\text{ in}$).
  \item \textbf{Valid extreme domain outliers:} Extreme right-tail values in \texttt{n\_listings} (up to $4,959$), \texttt{price\_median} (up to $\$236,438$), \texttt{horsepower\_median} (up to $630\text{ HP}$), \texttt{city\_fuel\_economy\_median} (up to $126\text{ MPGe}$ for EV/hybrid dealers), and \texttt{daysonmarket\_median} (up to $1,987\text{ days}$) represent genuine market segments (mega-franchises, exotic/luxury dealers, and collector inventories). All $13,843$ sellers are therefore retained, and heavy right tails are compressed via $\log(1+x)$ transformation in Section~\ref{sec:transformation}.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Variables transformation}
\label{sec:transformation}

To mitigate severe right-skewness and resolve magnitude disparities across attributes, we applied logarithmic transformation (\texttt{np.log1p}) followed by feature scaling (\texttt{StandardScaler} and \texttt{MinMaxScaler}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Logarithmic tail compression (\texttt{np.log1p}):} Fifteen non-negative numerical features exhibited strong right-skewness ($\text{Skewness} > 1.2$). Because variables such as \texttt{savings\_amount\_median}, \texttt{daysonmarket\_median}, and \texttt{vehicle\_age\_median} include valid zero values where $\log(0)$ is undefined, we applied $\log(1 + x)$. As reported in Table~\ref{tab:skewness}, \texttt{np.log1p} restores near-symmetry ($|\text{Skewness}| < 1.0$) across all $9$ unbounded continuous scale, price, turnover, age, and powertrain variables. Conversely, for zero-inflated bounded proportions in $[0, 1]$ (\texttt{pct\_theft\_title}, \texttt{pct\_salvage}, \texttt{pct\_isCab}), $\ln(1+x) \approx x$ cannot eliminate the point mass at zero; these attributes are kept on their native $[0, 1]$ scale.
  \item \textbf{Feature scaling (\texttt{StandardScaler} vs.\ \texttt{MinMaxScaler}):} Raw features differ by up to five orders of magnitude (\texttt{mileage\_median} $\in [0,\, 238,541]$ vs.\ \texttt{pct\_*} $\in [0, 1]$). To prevent large-scale features from dominating Euclidean distances in Clustering and KNN, we constructed both Z-score standardized ($\mu = 0, \sigma = 1$) and Min-Max normalized ($[0, 1]$) matrices. Because Z-score standardization on untransformed skewed variables still leaves extreme maxima (e.g., $+35.32\,\sigma$ for \texttt{n\_listings}), \texttt{np.log1p} compression is applied prior to scaling for distance-based models.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Pairwise correlations and variable elimination}
\label{sec:correlations}

To investigate inter-feature dependencies, we visualized a pairwise scatter plot matrix of key numerical attributes colored by \texttt{seller\_rating\_class} (Figure~\ref{fig:pairplot}) and computed the full Spearman rank correlation matrix ($r_s$) across all $24$ numerical variables (Figure~\ref{fig:corr_heatmap}). Spearman correlation was chosen over Pearson correlation because Figure~\ref{fig:pairplot} reveals pronounced \textbf{non-linear monotonic relationships}—most notably the exponential L-shaped depreciation decay of \texttt{price\_median} with respect to \texttt{mileage\_median} and \texttt{vehicle\_age\_median}—as well as heavy-tailed distributions to which rank correlation is robust.

Analysis of the correlation matrix identifies three near-collinear pairs ($|r_s| > 0.93$) and two broader domain blocks:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{\texttt{year\_median} $\leftrightarrow$ \texttt{vehicle\_age\_median} ($r_s = -0.9837$):} Because $\texttt{vehicle\_age\_median} = 2020.0 - \texttt{year\_median}$, retaining both introduces severe multicollinearity in regression and double-weights vehicle age in distance-based clustering. \textbf{Decision:} Drop \texttt{year\_median} and retain \texttt{vehicle\_age\_median} (a ratio-scale variable with $0.00$ \texttt{log1p} skewness).
  \item \textbf{\texttt{city\_fuel\_economy\_median} $\leftrightarrow$ \texttt{highway\_fuel\_economy\_median} ($r_s = +0.9356$):} Both features capture the same underlying powertrain efficiency dimension across ICE and EV inventories. \textbf{Decision:} Drop \texttt{highway\_fuel\_economy\_median} and retain \texttt{city\_fuel\_economy\_median}.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{n\_listings} $\leftrightarrow$ \texttt{n\_listing\_days} ($r_s = +0.9559$):} Total listing volume and active posting days are tightly coupled; while \texttt{n\_listing\_days} is retained in the general table for exploratory ratio creation, it is excluded alongside collinear variables during clustering feature selection.
  \item \textbf{Lifecycle and Powertrain correlation blocks ($0.71 \le |r_s| \le 0.93$):} \texttt{vehicle\_age\_median}, \texttt{mileage\_median} ($+0.9268$), \texttt{pct\_is\_new} ($-0.8671$), and \texttt{price\_median} ($-0.8154$) form a cohesive vehicle-lifecycle cluster, while \texttt{fuel\_tank\_volume\_median}, \texttt{horsepower\_median} ($+0.8049$), \texttt{engine\_displacement\_median} ($+0.7949$), and \texttt{length\_median} ($+0.7596$) form a physical-size and powertrain block (with \texttt{engine\_displacement\_median} also correlating strongly with \texttt{horsepower\_median} at $+0.7653$ and negatively with \texttt{city\_fuel\_economy\_median} at $-0.7626$). Both \texttt{mileage\_median} and \texttt{vehicle\_age\_median} are retained because their divergence captures annual usage intensity (miles driven per year) for supervised modeling.
\end{itemize}

In summary, eliminating \texttt{dominant\_wheel\_system} ($75.98\%$ missing), \texttt{sp\_id} (unique identifier), \texttt{year\_median}, and \texttt{highway\_fuel\_economy\_median} yields a final prepared dataset (\texttt{df\_final}) of \textbf{$13,843\text{ rows} \times 29\text{ columns}$} with zero missing values.
