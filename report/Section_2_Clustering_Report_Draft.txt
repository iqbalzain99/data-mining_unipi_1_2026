2.1. Feature Engineering, Feature Selection Scenarios, and Scaling Comparison
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To establish a rigorous geometric space for distance-based clustering, we first bridged our correlation findings from Section 1.3.4 by constructing three domain-specific features before comparing four candidate feature subsets and two normalization schemes (Min-Max [0, 1] vs. Z-Score Standardization).

First, regarding Feature Engineering, in Section 1.3.4 we retained the strongly collinear pairs (n_listings, n_listing_days; rs = 0.96) and (mileage_median, vehicle_age_median; rs = 0.93) in the master table because their relative ratios capture operational velocity and annual vehicle wear. However, feeding both raw pairs directly into Euclidean distance calculations would double-count inventory scale and vehicle age, while keeping the three sparse history percentages (pct_isCab, pct_salvage, pct_theft_title) as separate axes introduces zero-inflated noise. We therefore engineered three compact features:
1. listings_per_day = n_listings / max(n_listing_days, 1): Measures active posting velocity (median = 1.79 listings/day; range = 1.00 to 53.38), distinguishing rapid-turnover dealerships from slow-moving lots while resolving the 0.96 collinearity with n_listing_days.
2. annual_mileage = mileage_median / (vehicle_age_median + 1): Captures annual vehicle utilization (median = 8,878 miles/year), separating brand-new inventory (approx. 9 miles/year) and lightly driven Certified Pre-Owned (CPO) lease returns (approx. 6,660 miles/year) from heavily utilized commercial fleet vehicles (> 12,500 miles/year), resolving the 0.93 collinearity with vehicle_age_median.
3. adverse_history_index = pct_isCab + pct_salvage + pct_theft_title: Aggregates commercial/taxi fleet history (non-zero in 87.1% of sellers) and severe title branding (salvage and theft recovery) into a single continuous index (median = 0.125; max = 1.396), compressing three separate columns into one axis.
Consistent with Section 1.3.3, np.log1p was applied to skewed continuous attributes before scaling.

Second, we benchmarked four Feature Space Scenarios across K-Means (k = 2 to 6) under both Min-Max and Z-Score scaling (Figure 2.1):
- Scenario 1 (Baseline - All 22 Original Features): Suffers from the curse of dimensionality, collinear double-weighting, and uninformative physical dimensions (length, width, height, legroom), yielding low Silhouette scores (0.349 at k = 3; 0.247 at k = 4 under Min-Max).
- Scenario 2 (8 Selected Core Original Features - n_listings, price_median, mileage_median, daysonmarket_median, pct_is_new, pct_isCab, horsepower_median, city_fuel_economy_median): Eliminating redundant and sparse columns raises the k = 4 Silhouette score from 0.247 to 0.371 (Davies-Bouldin drops from 1.57 to 1.14).
- Scenario 3A (9 Features) & Scenario 3B (7 Orthogonal Engineered & Core Features - listings_per_day, price_median, annual_mileage, pct_is_new, adverse_history_index, horsepower_median, city_fuel_economy_median): Replacing collinear pairs and sparse history flags with the three engineered features achieves the strongest cluster separation across all k values: in Scenario 3B (Min-Max), Silhouette reaches 0.571 at k = 2 (DB = 0.63), 0.471 at k = 3 (DB = 0.89), 0.399 at k = 4 (DB = 1.02), and 0.348 at k = 5 (DB = 1.07).
- Scenario 4 (PCA Dimensionality Reduction - 4 Principal Components explaining 85.3% of variance on the 22 features): Achieves Silhouette = 0.363 at k = 4 and 0.324 at k = 5. Notably, Scenario 3B outperforms PCA-4 while preserving 100% direct physical interpretability in original business units.

Finally, comparing Min-Max [0, 1] against Z-Score Standardization reveals that Min-Max consistently outperforms Z-Score across every scenario (e.g., k = 4 Silhouette of 0.399 vs. 0.241 in Scenario 3B). Because Z-Score leaves unbounded extreme values (up to 6-10 standard deviations on tail ratios), Euclidean distances become dominated by small outlier pockets, whereas Min-Max bounds every feature strictly within [0, 1]. Consequently, Scenario 3B with log1p + Min-Max scaling is selected as the primary feature space for subsequent clustering analyses.


2.2. Centroid-Based and Model-Based Clustering (K-Means, Bisecting K-Means, X-Means, GMM)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
We applied K-Means (k-means++ initialization, 20 runs) across k = 2 to 9 on Scenario 3B (Figure 2.2). To verify that the discovered clusters reflect genuine market structure rather than random partitioning of a continuous space, we performed a Monte Carlo Uniform Random SSE Significance Test (15 uniform random realizations over [0, 1]^7). At k = 4, the empirical SSE is 755.2 compared to 5,842.3 +/- 18.4 for uniform random data (Z = -276.5, p < 0.0001, an 87.1% SSE reduction), confirming highly significant cluster structure.

Evaluating the internal metrics across k (Figure 2.2a-c):
- k = 2 (SSE = 1,244.6; Silhouette = 0.571; DB = 0.630) captures the macro division between Franchise New/Mixed Dealerships (42.8%) and Independent Used Dealerships (57.2%).
- k = 3 (SSE = 880.7; Silhouette = 0.471; DB = 0.886) splits Franchise dealers into Pure New-Car Dealerships (28.3%) and Balanced New/CPO Dealerships (15.4%).
- k = 4 (SSE = 755.2; Silhouette = 0.399; DB = 1.022; CH = 16,571.5) and k = 5 (SSE = 666.9; Silhouette = 0.348; DB = 1.067; CH = 14,531.7) mark the primary elbow inflection zone before Silhouette drops sharply to 0.262 at k = 6.

Inspecting the inverse-transformed real-unit cluster medians and Parallel Coordinates (Figure 2.3) alongside external validation against unseen categorical attributes (Figure 2.4) reveals five distinct automotive dealer archetypes at k = 5 (with k = 4 merging C2 and C4 into a single Standard Used cluster):
1. Cluster 0 - High-Volume Pure New-Car Franchise Dealerships (28.3%, n = 3,914): Highest inventory volume (191 listings) and posting velocity (2.59 listings/day), highest median price ($30,093), delivery-level annual mileage (8.75 miles/year), and 68% new cars. External validation confirms 100.0% are Franchise dealers, predominantly selling SUVs/Crossovers (79.0%) with high seller ratings.
2. Cluster 3 - Balanced New & CPO Lease-Return Franchise Dealerships (14.5%, n = 2,013): Large inventory (142 listings; 2.20 listings/day) and high median price ($28,597), but moderate new-car share (45%) paired with low annual wear (6,656 miles/year), characteristic of Certified Pre-Owned lease-return programs. External validation shows 100.0% are Franchise dealers and 100.0% have a mixed new/used profile.
3. Cluster 1 - Commercial Fleet, Rental & Adverse-Title Used Specialists (7.8%, n = 1,081 at k = 5; 10.1%, n = 1,397 at k = 4): Medium-sized used lots (46 listings; $18,039 median price; 0% new cars) distinguished by the highest annual utilization (12,531 miles/year), elevated fuel economy (21.5 MPG, 217 HP compact/midsize fleet sedans), and an adverse_history_index of 0.46 (nearly 4x higher than all other clusters).
4. Cluster 2 - Economy / Budget Independent Used-Car Lots (31.1%, n = 4,308): Smallest scale (25 listings) and slowest velocity (1.35 listings/day), lowest median price ($9,773), high mileage wear (11,387 miles/year), and smaller engines (200.25 HP, 20.0 MPG). External validation confirms 94.0% are Independent dealers and 97.9% are used-only, with the highest share of sedans.
5. Cluster 4 - High-Power Pickup Truck/SUV & Premium Used Specialists (18.3%, n = 2,527): Mid-sized used dealers (38 listings; $21,500 median price) with the cleanest title history (adverse_history_index = 0.08), highest median horsepower (285.0 HP), and lowest fuel economy (18.0 MPG), corresponding to dealers specializing in Pickup Trucks and large V6/V8 SUVs.

We further compared K-Means against Bisecting K-Means, Pelleg-Moore X-Means, and Gaussian Mixture Models (GMM / EM) (Figure 2.5):
- Bisecting K-Means (biggest_inertia strategy) matches K-Means exactly at k = 2 (SSE = 1,244.6; Sil = 0.571) and closely tracks it at k = 4 (SSE = 826.2; Sil = 0.363; ARI = 0.812 with K-Means), though its greedy top-down splits cannot re-adjust boundaries globally, resulting in ~9% higher SSE at k = 4 and k = 5.
- X-Means (BIC) and GMM (Full Covariance) show rapid BIC improvements from k = 2 (-263,502) through k = 4 (-281,626) and k = 5 (-284,684) before flattening. Full-covariance GMM achieves Silhouette = 0.388 at k = 4 (vs. 0.296 for Diagonal GMM), demonstrating that accommodating elliptical covariance between price, horsepower, and fuel economy improves mixture fit.


2.3. Density-Based Clustering (DBSCAN, OPTICS, and HDBSCAN)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To evaluate non-parametric clustering without spherical assumptions, we applied DBSCAN, OPTICS, and HDBSCAN to Scenario 3B (Figure 2.6).

First, we plotted sorted k-nearest-neighbor distance curves for k in {5, 10, 15, 20, 30} (Figure 2.6a). Across k = 15 to 30, a clear knee inflection occurs within eps in [0.09, 0.11], where the curve transitions from the dense core distribution (80th-85th percentile) to the steep outlier tail. A systematic grid search over eps in [0.08, 0.12] and min_samples in {10, 15, 20, 30} (Figure 2.6b) identifies two key operational regimes:
- At eps = 0.10, min_samples = 20, DBSCAN extracts 3 core clusters (sizes = [8,568 Used Core; 2,926 Pure New Franchise Core; 75 CPO Bridge]) while flagging 16.4% of sellers (n = 2,274) as noise, achieving a high non-noise Silhouette score of 0.547.
- Reducing the radius to eps = 0.09, min_samples = 20 resolves 5 density clusters (sizes = [7,013, 2,302, 1,064, 86, 68]) with 23.9% noise and a clean Silhouette score of 0.401, successfully separating the Balanced CPO Franchise group (n = 1,064) from the Pure New Franchise core (n = 2,302).

Second, to overcome DBSCAN's sensitivity to a single global eps threshold across variable-density regions, we computed the OPTICS Reachability Plot (min_samples = 25, xi = 0.04; Figure 2.6c) and applied HDBSCAN (min_cluster_size = 100, min_samples = 20; Figure 2.6d). The OPTICS reachability profile clearly reveals why fixed-eps DBSCAN struggles at eps = 0.10: the Pure New Franchise valley (reachability ~ 0.04-0.06) and Independent Used valley (reachability ~ 0.05-0.08) are substantially denser than the Balanced CPO Franchise valley (reachability ~ 0.08-0.11). HDBSCAN automatically adapts to these variable density levels, stably recovering all 3 major market regimes across min_cluster_size in [100, 200] (Cluster 2 Used Core: n = 6,560; Cluster 0 Pure New Franchise: n = 2,662; Cluster 1 Balanced CPO Franchise: n = 669) with 28.5% noise and a clean Silhouette score of 0.524.


2.4. Hierarchical Clustering (Agglomerative)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
We next performed Agglomerative Hierarchical Clustering on all 13,843 sellers across four linkage criteria (Ward, Complete, Average, Single) and two distance metrics (Euclidean L2 and Manhattan L1), evaluating both tree faithfulness via the Cophenetic Correlation Coefficient (c) and partition quality at k = 2 to 5 (Figure 2.7):
1. Ward Linkage (Euclidean, c = 0.8741; Figure 2.7a): Minimizing within-cluster variance produces a well-balanced dendrogram with sharp vertical merge jumps. Remarkably, at k = 4, Ward linkage achieves a higher Silhouette score (0.445) and lower Davies-Bouldin index (0.88) than K-Means (0.399 and 1.02), partitioning the market into Independent Used (n = 7,115), Pure New Franchise (n = 4,004), Balanced CPO Franchise (n = 2,234), and Commercial/Adverse-Title Specialists (n = 490). Cutting further at k = 5 (Sil = 0.312) splits the 7,115 Used dealers into Budget Used (n = 4,471) and High-Power Truck/SUV Used (n = 2,644).
2. Complete Linkage (Euclidean c = 0.8323 vs. Manhattan c = 0.8153; Figure 2.7b): Enforces compact cluster diameters. Notably, Manhattan (L1) Complete Linkage outperforms Euclidean (L2) Complete Linkage at k = 4 (Sil = 0.441 vs. 0.415; sizes = [7,591, 3,576, 2,575, 101]) and k = 5 (Sil = 0.401 vs. 0.368), because L1 distance is less distorted by single-feature extremes when computing maximum pairwise inter-cluster distances.
3. Average Linkage / UPGMA (Euclidean c = 0.8998, Manhattan c = 0.8737; Figure 2.7c): Achieves the highest Cophenetic Correlation of all methods, indicating that average inter-group distances most faithfully preserve pairwise topological distances. At k = 2, it matches Ward linkage almost identically (Sil = 0.572, sizes = [9,840, 4,003]); however, at k = 3 to 5, it keeps the two macro clusters intact while isolating small extreme outlier groups (n = 38, 10, 9), acting as a structural compromise between Complete and Single linkage.
4. Single Linkage (Euclidean c = 0.5372, Manhattan c = 0.5951; Figure 2.7d): Exhibits severe chaining effect, merging 13,842 sellers into one giant contiguous chain while peeling off single anomalous dealers one by one (sizes = [13,840, 1, 1, 1] at k = 4; Sil = 0.275), confirming its unsuitability for continuous multi-dimensional dealer segmentation.

Concluding Insights for the Seller Dataset:
1. Algorithm Typology Suitability: Because automotive seller attributes (pricing, annual mileage, posting velocity, horsepower) form continuous, partially overlapping regimes rather than disconnected islands in R^7, variance-minimizing partitioning and hierarchical algorithms (K-Means and Ward Agglomerative Clustering at k = 4 and k = 5) provide the most practical and exhaustive segmentation for business profiling.
2. Role of Density-Based Methods: DBSCAN and HDBSCAN complement K-Means and Ward by proving that the Seller dataset contains three high-density gravitational cores (Independent Used, Pure New Franchise, and Balanced CPO Franchise) surrounded by a ~16%-28% halo of specialized/hybrid dealers (such as Commercial Fleet/Adverse-Title specialists and exotic/high-power truck boutiques).
3. Impact of Feature Engineering & Cross-Scenario Ablation (Figure 2.9): To verify that our attribute selection is not biased toward spherical K-Means, we further benchmarked Scenario 2A (7 Core Original Attributes: n_listings, price_median, mileage_median, pct_is_new, pct_isCab, horsepower_median, city_fuel_economy_median), Scenario 2B (8 Original Attributes, adding daysonmarket_median), and Scenario 3B (7 Engineered & Core Attributes) across all clustering families. First, adding daysonmarket_median (Scenario 2B) consistently degraded performance across all methods (e.g., HDBSCAN noise jumped from 34.6% to 52.5%). Second, Scenario 2A (7 Core Original) proved competitive in K-Means at k = 4 (Sil = 0.388 vs. 0.399 in 3B; franchise_status NMI = 0.523) and Euclidean Complete Linkage (Sil = 0.445 at k = 4), segmenting used-car lots primarily by cumulative mileage (40k vs. 115k miles). However, Scenario 3B outperformed Scenario 2A across the remaining paradigms: it boosted Ward k = 4 Silhouette by +56.2% (from 0.283 to 0.442), improved hierarchical Cophenetic Correlation from 0.7944 to 0.8741, prevented DBSCAN density-bridging at eps = 0.10 (All-Silhouette 0.359 vs. 0.107), enabled HDBSCAN to recover the third CPO Franchise core with lower noise (28.5% vs. 34.6%), and achieved the highest external agreement with new_used_profile (Ward k = 4 NMI = 0.681, ARI = 0.684).
